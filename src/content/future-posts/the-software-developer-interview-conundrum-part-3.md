## The Do's and Dont's of software interviews

As a preamble, a gentle reminder that an interview is (or at least should be) both ways. You are interviewing the candidates as much as they are interviewing you.

> Interviewee: you must use an interview as a mean to figure out whether the company is a fit for you. An interview is never only about the company getting to know you.

Let's get started with the Do's

### Do's

#### Do: respect the candidate

Respect the candidate throughout the interview process.

This starts before you even get a resume. Prepare the interview process before you even start putting job ads out.

- Why do we interview in the first place?
- How many interviews in the process?
- What do each interview look like?
- Who interviews when?
- Define a maximum time for the process, the shorter the better, don't let it drag over many weeks/months

In general once an interview process is started, stick to that process until you filled the position. Candidates need to be treated equally.

Now your job ad is out, you start receiving resumes. Don't let them linger in your inbox, review them in a timely manner. While reviewing make sure that the person matches what you are looking for. The goal here is to avoid wasting anyone's time.

Interviewees offer you their time to go through your interview process. They deserve feedback and deserve it early. You've been there and you've complained about it. Don't be that person you complained about.

Oh, and "We've decided to go with another candidate at this time" is not feedback. Always give areas of improvement to the candidate.

#### Do: come prepared to the interview

They have done a take home? Review it thoroughly before the interview. They've spent the time doing, you owe them to spend the time reviewing. Respect, remember?

Before the interview, have another look at their resume, to avoid asking questions that are already answered.

#### Do: put the candidate at ease

This starts before the interview by letting them know what is expected of them during the interview.

- Explain the interview structure
- Tell them what they are and what they are not allowed during the interview. Can they google? Can they use AI? Let it be known to avoid misunderstandings

At the start of the interview, reiterate those points, before moving on to the interview.

#### Do: leave room for questions

I've said this before. An interview goes both way, you are interviewing as much as you are interviewed. The candidate should walk away from the interview with a pretty clear idea of how the company and team operates, as well as knowing whether they are a good fit

#### Do: be honest in your answers

#### Do: have a swift process

Do leave room for questions. An interview should ideally be both ways. You are making sure the interviewee is fit for the position, but the interviewee should walk away from the interview process with a pretty clear idea of where is going to land if the accepts the job offer.

Do be honest when you answer questions. There is nothing worse than promoting a lie to get someone to sign. They will realise you were lying in the first few days and leave the company. I once unwillingly lied and hired someone as a technical lead, while I was supposed to transition to an official engineering manager position that I was already doing, leading 2 teams. That transition was cancelled a week after hiring the tech lead. That tech lead was given 4 people to manage, when he clearly told us that he did not want to manage people. And ended up leaving the company 3 weeks later.

Do put the candidate at ease. As I mentioned above this is a both way process, you interview them as much as they interview you. So when you say you can use google, mean it. And show it. If it is a pair programming session actually pair, help them when they struggle, build some momentum so that they forget they are in an interview.

### Don'ts

#### Don't: falsely advertise your interview style

#### Don't: lie about take home test length

#### Don't: be late at the interview

Seriously, don't

#### Don't: force a coding environment

#### Don't: throw curve balls

#### Don't: let the interview go over

#### Don't: ask non-relevant questions

Most of what we will discuss in this point revolves around keeping the interview relevant to your needs.

I'll start with what I think is the absolute worst. Don't ask brain teasers. Why not? They are simply not relevant to the job. You might miss on a very good developer because they could not answer a puzzle. I would push it even further, you could exclude full groups of people with your brain teasers. People with ADHD, autism or dyslexia might struggle with brain teasers. So please, if you are still using brain teasers in your interview process, stop doing it.

Also don't ask questions about how a software or a framework works under the hood. While I personally find it interesting to know how the tools I use work, it is not necessary to make you good at using them. Woodworkers don't need to know how their saws are build. So why should a react developer know how react is built? Unless of course you hire someone to work on the react codebase!

No one in your company has ever inverted a binary tree in production code? Don't ask the candidate to invert a binary tree. Keep the interview focused on what matters for the position.

#### Don't: ask candidates to do actual work

#### Don't: ghost candidates

Don't advertise an interview as a pairing session when it is just a disguised coding interview.

Don't say your take home test takes two hours when it needs a weekend to complete. Candidates want to show their best side and will spend the time to do so. Even if you tell them to do it in two hours, they'll spend more time. Cater for this and if you say something takes two hours it's because your team all took just one hour to complete it.

Don't do it on a code sandbox or an environment the interviewee is not used to. But Jon, they will have intellisense! Yeah, they will when they work also. One caveat here, I would still ask anyone to turn off their AI helpers during a live coding session. AI helpers write the code for you, sometimes substantial amount of it, so it becomes difficult for me to assess if the person knows how to write proper code without an AI helper.

Don't throw curve balls. I know that some companies do this. I understand that this helps figuring out how people react when they are under pressure. This does not take into account the current pressure they are on. As an interviewer the current pressure is something that you need to gauge before throwing a curve ball.

Don't ask candidates to work on an actual production issue. Whatever the type of interview you are doing.

Don't ghost candidates after they interviewed with you. If you realise that a mistake happened in the process and you ghosted them as a result, send an apology.
